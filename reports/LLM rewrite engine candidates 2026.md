# Six cheap challengers to test against Flash-Lite

> Research date 2026-09-26. Built from six research-note files (Western closed APIs, Chinese labs, Chinese challengers, open-weight models and hosts, near-zero-cost options, quality benchmarks). **Every $/piece figure is recomputed on RetAIn's measured production token mix**: 10,227 input + 1,594 output tokens per piece across 3–4 calls (rewrite ~2,600/700, two judges ~1,450/50 each, sometimes a repair ~660/80). Formula: $/piece = 10,227 × input price/1M + 1,594 × output price/1M. **Costs exclude reasoning tokens.** If a model still "thinks", those hidden tokens are billed as output and add to the cost. Claims marked **(unverified)** come from search snippets, secondary sources or inference.

**Test six models, all priced under today's $0.00495 per piece: gpt-6-luna with reasoning off ($0.00182, 63% cheaper), gemini-2.5-flash-lite ($0.00166, 66% cheaper), NVIDIA nemotron-3.5-lightning ($0.00114, 77% cheaper), Gemma 4 26B-A4B ($0.00105, 79% cheaper), DeepSeek V4.1-Flash on a US host with thinking off ($0.00210, 58% cheaper), and gpt-5.4-nano ($0.00404, 18% cheaper).** Together they cover the three things being optimised. Luna, 2.5 Flash-Lite and Nemotron have sub-second time to first token. 2.5 Flash-Lite, nano and Gemma 4 have public hallucination rates of 3.1–5.2%, against 8.2% for the incumbent. DeepSeek V4.1-Flash lets RetAIn test Chinese-lab quality without sending text to China. Cost barely separates them. At today's volume of about 150 pieces a month, the incumbent costs about **$0.74/month** and the cheapest serious challenger about $0.16, so the most any switch can save today is roughly 60 cents a month. Cost starts to matter only at scale: at 100,000 pieces a month it is $495 against $105–$210 for five of the six challengers. **Quality therefore decides the winner, and no public benchmark measures RetAIn's quality bar**, which is placing a word only where it is idiomatic. The shortlist only decides what gets tested. The founder's blind bake-off decides what ships. Four newer challengers (Xiaomi MiMo-V2.6-Flash, Inception Mercury 2.5, Ant Ling-3.0-flash, Alibaba qwen3.7-flash) are cheap enough that the automated screen can include them for about a cent each. They sit on a reserve bench because each has either no speed data, no documented off-switch, or only a China-based route. **Out immediately**: everything over the cap, including gemini-3.5-flash-lite ($0.00705), and every model whose thinking cannot be switched off.

## The shortlist: six models under the cap, three with verified off-switches

Terms used below. **TTFT** (time to first token) is how long before the first word appears, which is what the reader feels when RetAIn streams a rewrite. **tok/s** is output speed once text is flowing. **Thinking** (or reasoning) is hidden text a model generates before answering. It is billed as output and delays the first word. The **Vectara hallucination rate** is the share of document summaries in which a model adds claims that are not in the source. It is the closest public proxy for RetAIn's "don't invent facts" rule, though it scores 55–250-word summaries, not 300–3,000-word rewrites ([Vectara leaderboard](https://github.com/vectara/hallucination-leaderboard), updated 2026-09-22). **ZDR** (zero data retention) means the host keeps no copy of prompts or outputs.

Each slot has a job. **Luna** is the only brand-new model below the cap with a documented, complete reasoning off-switch, and its 0.87 s TTFT beats the incumbent's measured figure by a wide margin. It has no quality evidence of any kind. AA's note that Luna hallucinated less than its predecessor applies to the *max* effort setting, not `none` ([AA article](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/gpt-6-sol-and-luna-push-the-cost-efficiency-frontier)). **gemini-2.5-flash-lite** is the safest bet on paper: same SDK, verified off-switch, the lowest TTFT AA tracks, and a hallucination rate less than half the incumbent's. It is also the control that shows whether the 3.x generation bought RetAIn anything. **Nemotron 3.5 Lightning** is the best-balanced upstart: fast, 77% cheaper, and also offered free on OpenRouter. **Gemma 4 26B-A4B** is the cheapest model on the list with a good faithfulness score, from the incumbent's own lab under Apache 2.0. **DeepSeek V4.1-Flash** is the fastest cheap open-weight model with a verified off-switch, and on a US host it keeps data out of China. **gpt-5.4-nano** has the best faithfulness score of any small model. It stays on the list despite the thin 18% saving because a more faithful rewriter could take work off the fact judge.

Two costs on this table carry a caveat. DeepSeek V4.1-Flash on DeepInfra is fp8, meaning weights are stored at reduced precision. The same model on Together is $0.00498, just over the cap, so the host has to be pinned. The speed figures for DeepSeek were measured with reasoning on, so thinking-off speed should be faster, but that is unmeasured.

## Free tiers and discounts cut pennies but cost privacy

Free and discounted routes exist, but at current volume they save less than a dollar a month and each carries a condition. **The Gemini API free tier covers the incumbent itself, but Google marks every free-tier model as "used to improve our products"** outside the EU, UK and EEA ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). That is fine for the founder's own testing and a blocker once other people's reading passes through it. Google does not publish free-tier rate limits. Secondary sources give about 15–30 requests per minute and 500–1,000 per day for Flash-Lite, and report cuts of 50–80% in December 2025 **(unverified)** ([Gemini rate limits](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/rate-limits); [tokenmix](https://tokenmix.ai/blog/gemini-api-free-tier-limits)). OpenRouter's `:free` models, which include nemotron-3.5-lightning and Gemma 4, allow **50 requests a day, or 1,000 after a one-off $10 credit purchase**, at 20 per minute ([OpenRouter limits](https://openrouter.ai/docs/api/reference/limits)). At 3–4 calls per piece that is about 12 or 250 pieces a day. The logging policy of the providers behind each free model was not checked. Mistral's free tier requires opting into training. Groq's 6,000 tokens-per-minute free cap is below one piece's 11,800 tokens, so a single piece would span two minutes **(secondary source, unverified)** ([OpenRouter blog](https://openrouter.ai/blog/tutorials/free-llm-apis-compared/)). Inception gives **100M free tokens** with Mercury 2.5, about 8,500 pieces at RetAIn's mix ([Inception blog](https://www.inceptionlabs.ai/blog/introducing-mercury-2-5)).

On paid routes, Gemini's Batch and Flex tiers are 50% off, which puts the incumbent at **$0.00247 per piece**. Luna's Flex tier would be $0.00091. Batch is asynchronous and cannot serve a live streamed rewrite. Whether Flex adds queuing delay was not checked ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing); [OpenAI gpt-6-luna](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-6-luna)). Cached input (reusing the shared system prompt) saves fractions of a tenth of a cent per piece. The cacheable share of RetAIn's prompts is unmeasured.

## China-hosted routes are a privacy decision; Western ZDR hosts avoid it

**Open-weight** means the lab publishes the model file, so any host can run it. That separates the model's origin from where the data goes. DeepSeek V4.1-Flash and GLM-5.3-Flash are MIT-licensed ([AA comparison](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/comparisons/deepseek-v4-1-flash-vs-glm-5-3-flash)), MiMo-V2.6-Flash is MIT ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/XiaomiMiMo/MiMo-V2.6-Flash-RL)), and US hosts such as DeepInfra, Fireworks and Together serve them at or below the labs' own prices. The first-party routes are a different matter. **DeepSeek's privacy policy says it stores personal data "in People's Republic of China"** and may train on it, with an opt-out ([DeepSeek privacy policy](https://cdn.deepseek.com/policies/en-US/deepseek-privacy-policy.html)). Its API terms are governed by PRC law and name no data location ([DeepSeek API terms](https://cdn.deepseek.com/policies/en-US/deepseek-open-platform-terms-of-service.html)). Alibaba runs Qwen in US, Singapore and Frankfurt regions and says it "will never use your data for model training". That comes from search snippets, not the fetched page **(unverified)** ([Alibaba privacy notice](https://www.alibabacloud.com/help/en/model-studio/privacy-notice)). The vendor is still headquartered in China. The data terms of Xiaomi, inclusionAI (Ling), Meituan, StepFun, Tencent and ByteDance were not retrieved. Treat their first-party APIs as unknown. US restrictions found so far cover government devices, not consumer apps ([GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/biz/data/wheres-deepseek-banned-the-states-blocking-chinese-made-ai)). EU users' text reaching China would need a GDPR cross-border transfer mechanism, because China has no EU adequacy decision **(inference, not researched)**.

The rule for RetAIn is to run Chinese-lab models only as open weights on a Western host that offers zero data retention. **OpenRouter can enforce ZDR per request or across an account**, and Groq offers it self-serve ([OpenRouter ZDR](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/zdr); [Groq data docs](https://console.groq.com/docs/your-data)). ZDR terms for DeepInfra, Fireworks and Together specifically were not verified. Three models on the lists have no Western route at all: qwen3.7-flash, qwen3.8-flash and Seed-2.0-mini are served only by Alibaba or ByteDance. Cheap Western endpoints often run quantized weights (fp4 or fp8), which can degrade prose, so the bake-off should pin one provider and one precision per model ([OpenRouter DeepSeek V4.1-Flash endpoints](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models/deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash/endpoints)).

## A two-stage bake-off that spends founder time only on survivors

**Stage 1 is an automated screen that costs under 50 cents.** Run the incumbent, the six shortlisted models and the four reserves on about 8 real pieces taken from the 26 in production, through `src/bakeoff.py`, with the current prompt. Eleven models × 8 pieces at roughly $0.001–0.005 each comes to well under $0.50 at list price. Set every thinking flag explicitly: `reasoning_effort: none` for Luna, `thinking: {type: disabled}` for DeepSeek, `enable_thinking: false` for Qwen, Ling and MiMo, and OpenRouter's `reasoning` parameter set to off for Nemotron and Gemma. DeepSeek and Qwen think *by default*, so a run without the flag repeats July's Kimi failure (115 s, hidden tokens). Read the billed input, output and reasoning tokens from each response's usage fields, then recompute $/piece from what was billed rather than from list prices. Keep `max_tokens` at about 12K of headroom, per the July truncation lesson. A model is dropped automatically if its **measured** cost exceeds $0.00495 per piece, if it emits reasoning tokens after the off flag (which is how the unverified off-switches get verified), if rewrite TTFT exceeds about 2 s at p90, or if it breaks the output format twice. Run the existing fact judge over every output and count flagged inventions per piece as a first faithfulness signal.

**Stage 2 is a blind founder rating of the survivors.** Take the top three or four from Stage 1 plus the incumbent, and run 20–30 pieces × 2 generations each. That gives roughly 100–180 word decisions per model, enough to separate a 5% misuse rate from 15% but not from 8%. Compare models on the same pieces, and group uncertainty by source text ([Miller, arXiv 2411.00640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)). The founder rates with model names hidden and order shuffled, since swapping presentation order reverses about half of pairwise judgements ([arXiv 2609.17857](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17857)). Rate each target word: placed or skipped. If placed: idiomatic, acceptable-but-marked, or wrong collocation. If skipped: was there a natural slot? Then rate each piece: count of invented facts or attributions against the source, and whether the reading level was kept rather than simplified. Re-rate about 20% of items to check your own consistency. Agree the decision rule before the run: a challenger replaces the incumbent only if its misuse rate and invented-fact count are no worse and it wins on at least one, at a measured cost under $0.00495 and a streaming TTFT under about 2 s. Changing how the judges are graded is parked and out of scope here.

## Conclusion

The cost rule collapses the field faster than any benchmark. Once cost is measured per piece on the real token mix and thinking must switch off, fewer than a dozen models remain worth a human's time, and the survivors differ from each other by fractions of a cent. That turns a model-selection problem into a measurement problem. RetAIn does not need more leaderboard research. It needs Stage 1's billed-token logs, which settle the six unverified off-switches and the true costs in one run, and Stage 2's per-word ratings, which are the only evidence that matches the product's quality bar.

The split in public evidence points to one hypothesis worth watching. The models with the best faithfulness scores (nano, 2.5 Flash-Lite, Gemma 4, Phi-4) are older or smaller, while the fastest and newest (Luna, Nemotron Lightning, Mercury 2.5, MiMo) have no faithfulness data at all. If an older model with low hallucination rates matches the incumbent on idiom, the fact judge may be catching fewer errors, and RetAIn can drop to about a third of today's cost with less risk than a brand-new model brings. If only the newest models place words well, the judge stays essential whatever wins.
